Now you've potentially got three communication classes.

CPU/QPU optical channels — high-bandwidth classical traffic through the crystalline photonic substrate.

That starts looking less like a conventional CPU with quantum accessories and more like a heterogeneous compute fabric where classical processors orchestrate a network of quantum processors.

The crystal substrate then has a very specific reason to exist: it provides the high-bandwidth classical optical network and, if the chosen qubit technology permits it, potentially the photonic quantum-interconnect network.

The key research question becomes particularly sharp:

Can a classical processor dynamically partition a workload across multiple photonic-interconnected quantum processing modules while maintaining sufficiently low communication latency—and can the same substrate eventually provide coherent quantum links between those modules?

That is a much stronger POC thesis than simply "put qubits in crystal."
